01 Keynote 01 · Parents / Educators

Raising Humans in the Age of AI

What should we still teach kids to do when AI can do almost everything else?

Kids aren't simply growing up with technology anymore. They're growing up alongside systems that can write, create, explain, persuade and increasingly think alongside them.

This talk asks a harder question than "how much screen time is too much": what should we still teach children to do when technology can do so much for them? Drawing on 20+ years coaching young people, building technology products and leading youth-development programs, Shaun gives parents and educators a practical framework for raising young people who can use AI without outsourcing their judgment, effort, relationships or responsibility.

This isn't an anti-technology talk. It's a case for better humans in a world of better technology.

"Use AI. Don't outsource the thinking."

Audience: Parents · educators · schools · youth organizations  ·  Format: 45–60 min + Q&A
02 Keynote 02 · Sports / Youth Development

The Digital Athlete

Winning without losing the human game.

Today's young athletes live in a world of constant measurement: game film, rankings, recruiting profiles, social media, wearable data, AI analysis and instant feedback.

Technology can make athletes better. It can also make them more anxious, more comparative and more dependent on external validation. This talk explores how coaches, parents and sports organizations can use technology to enhance performance without letting it replace the human elements that make sport transformative: resilience, relationships, identity, judgment and joy.

When everything about an athlete can be measured, who is teaching the athlete who they are?

"Technology can measure performance. Coaches develop people."

Audience: Coaches · athletic departments · youth sports orgs · sports-tech · parents  ·  Format: 45–60 min + Q&A
03 Keynote 03 · Education

When AI Knows the Answer

What should teachers teach instead?

Generative AI has changed one of education's oldest assumptions: that access to information is difficult. Students can now generate explanations, essays and solutions in seconds — so what happens to education when getting the answer is no longer the hard part?

Shaun challenges educators to rethink what — and how — we teach, without allowing AI to replace productive struggle, curiosity, critical thinking and human relationships.

"When answers become cheap, thinking becomes more valuable."

Audience: Teachers · school leaders · edtech  ·  Format: 45–60 min + Q&A
04 Keynote 04 · Corporate / AI / Product

AI Can See the Pattern. Can It See the Person?

Human judgment in an age of increasingly capable machines.

AI is becoming extraordinarily good at seeing patterns — analyzing more information and generating recommendations at extraordinary speed. But recognizing a pattern isn't the same as understanding a person, a customer, an athlete or a problem.

Drawing from product leadership, AI development and two decades of working with people and organizations, Shaun argues the competitive advantage of the AI era belongs to organizations that know when to trust the model, when to challenge it, and when human judgment has to take over.

"AI can find the pattern. Humans still have to decide what it means."

Audience: Tech & product orgs · AI conferences · founders  ·  Format: 45–60 min + Q&A

One Point of View

The Human Game.

AI is changing what humans can do. The harder question is what humans still need to learn to do.

Shaun's work sits at that intersection: coaching young people, building technology, developing products and creating opportunities through sport and education. Across every keynote and workshop, the same principles return:

Judgment

Knowing what to believe, what to question and what to do.

Effort

Understanding that growth still requires struggle.

Relationships

Recognizing what technology cannot replace.

Responsibility

Owning what we do with increasingly powerful tools.

Better technology doesn't eliminate the need for better humans. It makes it more important.
